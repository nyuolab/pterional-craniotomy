Sources & model limits

Z-Anatomy, CC BY-SA 4.0. Upstream BodyParts3D: DBCLS, CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan. Native atlas relationships are preserved in one coordinate system.

These are educational atlas scenes with original illustrative bone openings, a local dural envelope, illustrative veins and arachnoid, and schematic motion. They are not article-specific reconstructions, validated operative simulations, or patient models. The dura is an illustrative sheet fitted under the inner table of the atlas skull. Deep and superficial temporal laminae and three fat pads are original illustrations built over the atlas temporalis; their thickness and extent are illustrative, not a native segmentation. Cortical parcels in the exposure are smoothed with an interpolating subdivision that removes faceting but adds no anatomical detail; the atlas gyral form is coarse, sulci appear as seams between separate parcels, and a darker illustrative backing fills those seams. Brain retraction is not modeled. The superficial Sylvian vein system, sphenoparietal and cavernous sinuses, superficial temporal vein, the variant Trolard and Labbé anastomotic veins (shown in Full head context) and the translucent arachnoid are original illustrations placed on the atlas anatomy; their courses are schematic, not segmented from imaging. The cavernous sinus is placed from skull-base landmarks (the atlas oculomotor and abducens nerves) because the atlas carotid runs lower than typical. The arachnoid is drawn milkier where it bridges the Sylvian fissure and is left out over the hidden orbital gyri and temporal pole. The arachnoid opening follows a schematic fissure line; opercular separation and the depth of the split are not reconstructed. Facial nerve branches, small cortical veins and perforators are not modeled. Colored markings, incision paths, burr holes, closure symbols and directional arrows are original schematic illustrations fitted to the reference anatomy, drawn as smooth lines resting just above the surface they mark (scalp, the exposed bed under the flap, bone or dura); they mark regions and sequences for teaching, not measured landmarks. The incision line draws in behind the opening cut, and the dural line follows the C-shaped dural cut. The scalp (skin with subcutaneous tissue, galea and pericranium) is an original illustration: offset shells fitted over the atlas skull, temporalis and illustrative temporal layers, about 7 mm thick over the vault, without hair, auricle or face. Its incision follows the upper part of the atlas schematic marking and is redrawn below it; markings do not establish a measured skin trajectory. Flaps (scalp, pericranium, temporal fascia, temporalis and the dural leaf) are peeled and rolled about straight illustrative hinges in the renderer, and the reflected scalp-layer flaps are laid toward the face; they are not soft-tissue simulations. The superficial temporal artery and vein are drawn in the subcutaneous layer where the illustrative scalp covers them (their atlas course lies deeper). Sterile drapes are not reconstructed. Patient positioning turns the whole atlas head rigidly about an estimated craniocervical pivot (between the occipital condyles); the neck, body and table tilt are not modeled. The head holder, its pin sites, the table edge and the orientation gizmo are schematic drawings, not measured equipment positions. Left-sided scalp layers, dura, fascial layers, fat pads, veins and arachnoid are fitted to native left anatomy by the same method (the scalp reuses the right cut layout); left markings are redrawn on native left anatomy (the deep Sylvian, clinoid and optico-carotid markings and the tools mirror the right illustrations), and native left-sided atlas structures retain their own geometry. Cyan instruments show qualitative entry directions rather than measured trajectories. The clinoid detail isolates the sphenoid, arteries, nerves and the sinuses beside it; calvaria, local lateral dura, cortex and all but the last centimetre of the superficial Sylvian vein are omitted in this view, not removed by clinoidectomy. Full head context expands the enabled layers to the available head atlas structures; from the burr holes to bone replacement it also shows the reflected scalp, pericranium, temporal fascia and temporalis in their current state. Each layer can also be expanded separately with its All checkbox; this does not imply complete segmentation of every tissue. Amber marks the current illustrative change; Before/After preserves preceding steps.

Colors: the default Surgical palette approximates the appearance of tissue under an operating microscope (wet highlights, light falloff toward the edge of the field, cut bone showing pale inner and outer tables with pinkish diploë between them; that layering is estimated by the renderer, not modeled). Its fine surface patterns — bone porosity, muscle fibres, fascial streaks, fat lobules and very faint branching on the cortex — are procedural texture added for realism; they do not show real vessels or measured tissue structure, and no dural vessels are drawn. The thin suprafascial areolar layer is drawn translucent. Crease shading is precomputed ambient occlusion from the mesh geometry. The Atlas palette restores the original teaching colors, which separate tissue layers more clearly.

The muscle-splitting view retains intact reference muscle; an exact fiber split is not reconstructed. The plane diagram is an original schematic, not a published figure. Article text and bibliographic references are available in Papers.
